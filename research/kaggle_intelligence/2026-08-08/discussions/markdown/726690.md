Hey there,

Thanks for the detailed analysis! The observed disparity in episode rates is expected behavior. How submissions are scheduled for games:

The matchmaker runs every ~4 minutes and selects which submissions most "need" a new game. The need score is based on a submission's rating uncertainty (sigma) and time since last game (newer and more-stale submissions are scheduled more frequently), but it's also scaled by the submission's rating. Higher-rated submissions accumulate scheduling priority faster than lower-rated ones. This is by design: it helps top-of-leaderboard ratings converge more quickly.

To answer your specific questions:

1. **Do opponent-side games update rating?** Yes. When any episode is created, all participating submissions (both the "selected" agent and their opponent) have their ratings and uncertainty updated based on the outcome.

2. **How is the non-random opponent chosen?** Opponents are selected via weighted random sampling over a Gaussian window centered on the focal agent's score. Closer-rated agents are more likely to be selected as opponents.

3. **Is episode frequency dependent on rating?** Yes, but there's a minimum for all submissions. The scheduling formula includes a multiplier based on rating that ranges relative to the initial rating of 600. This means higher-rated agents are scheduled for games more frequently but all submissions are expected to see a minimum of 48 episodes per day.

4. **Is 6-10x disparity expected?** The multiplier alone accounts for about 8x difference in scheduling priority. Combined with the fact that high-rated agents are also frequently selected as opponents by other high-rated agents (which counts as additional games), 6-10x is within the expected range.

5. **Does this affect rating confidence?** More games mean faster convergence (lower sigma), so higher-rated agents do stabilize their ratings faster. However, our rating system is designed so that ratings still converge for all agents. It just takes longer for agents with fewer games.

6. **Final evaluation:** The competition's final evaluation phase will continue to generate new episodes to determine final standings with the primary goal of reducing uncertainty. Interim match count differences during the leaderboard phase do not determine final rankings.

We understand this can feel frustrating, especially for newer or lower-ranked submissions. The 10% random opponent pool (introduced June 30) helps ensure all submissions get some diversity in matchups and that any ranking meta "bands" still faced shakeups. We'll continue monitoring match rates across the competition.

Thank you for the detailed explanation. However, I have to be direct: I believe this intended scheduling design is fundamentally unsuitable for this competition.

First, the stated minimum of 48 episodes per day does not appear to be met.

For the complete calendar day of July 16 in Japan Standard Time:

- Submission 54703723 received 20 unique public episodes.
- Submission 54707588 received 29 unique public episodes.

Please clarify whether 48 episodes is a guaranteed minimum, a target average, or only an expected value, and how “per day” is defined.

More importantly, the disclosed scheduling system appears to create a path-dependent feedback loop:

early wins  
→ higher rating while uncertainty is high  
→ up to approximately 8x more scheduling priority  
→ more opponent-side games from nearby high-rated agents  
→ faster sigma reduction  
→ earlier stabilization at the higher rating

A submission that loses several early games follows the opposite path: lower scheduling priority, fewer recovery opportunities, and slower convergence.

A rating-dependent scheduling multiplier might be defensible in a competition that isolates agent quality—for example, if every agent used the same fixed deck, matches were played as best-of series, first/second-player balance was controlled, and evaluation conditions were comparable.

That is not the environment of this competition.

Participants may use any legal deck, matches are single games, and Pokémon TCG has substantial variance from opening hands, Prize cards, first/second-player order, and strongly non-transitive deck matchups.

The leaderboard therefore combines:

- agent decision quality,
- deck selection,
- metagame prediction,
- matchup distribution,
- game variance,
- early matchmaking results,
- and rating-dependent episode allocation.

Under these conditions, giving an early high-rated submission substantially more games does not merely make a reliable estimate converge faster. It may allow an initially favorable evaluation path to determine the submission’s future evaluation conditions.

Providing the official engine and sample agents is useful for local development, but it does not solve this live-evaluation problem. An improved submission can perform well locally, lose several early games around the initial 600–700 rating range due to normal variance or unfavorable matchups, and then receive far fewer opportunities to recover or demonstrate the improvement.

Participants cannot reliably distinguish between:

- a genuinely weaker agent,
- an improved agent that received an unfavorable early evaluation path,
- and a deck that happened to perform well against the initial rating-band meta.

The problem is therefore not simply that lower-rated submissions take longer to converge. The problem is that their evaluation conditions change as a consequence of the early rating outcome.

The final evaluation details are therefore essential. Could you please clarify:

1. Will rating and sigma be reset before final evaluation?
2. Will every final submission be evaluated to a common sigma threshold?
3. Will final submissions receive comparable match counts and opponent distributions?
4. What is the stopping condition?
5. Which submissions will be included?

Without common evaluation conditions, I do not understand how interim matchmaking paths and the disclosed 6–10x episode-rate disparity can be prevented from affecting the final standings.
