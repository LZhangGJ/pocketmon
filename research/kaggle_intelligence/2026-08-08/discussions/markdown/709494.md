## 1. Watch out for private sharing

The most common source of trouble on Kaggle is "private sharing." The idea is very simple:

> **Anything about the substance of the competition (ideas, code, strategies, deck recipes, etc.) is either shared with everyone or with no one. Quietly sharing it with only a select few is NOT allowed.**

This rule exists to prevent the unfairness of having a small group hold information that gives them an advantage.

### Examples that are OK

- Sharing **your own score or rank**, e.g. "I reached Xth place!" or "New personal best!"
- Sharing impressions like "This competition is so much fun!" or cheering each other on
- General questions or chat about the competition spec or publicly available information

### Examples that are NOT OK

- Telling **only specific people your ideas or deck recipes** via DMs or off-team chats (Discord, LINE, etc.)
- Quietly trading strategies with someone who isn't on your team
- Slipping your tactics to a limited circle of friends as an "off-the-record" tip

The key point is that **"quietly, to only a select few" is the dangerous part.** Sharing your rank or score is fine, but handing the substance of your approach (ideas or recipes) to only a chosen few is out.

### Please bring useful information to the Kaggle discussions!

Here's the part not to misunderstand: it's **not that sharing information itself is bad.** Quite the opposite—**public sharing on the Kaggle platform is welcomed and encouraged.** Kaggle's official rules even state that if you do share, you should share it on the Kaggle discussion forum or notebooks for the benefit of all participants. As long as you share in a place everyone can see, there's no problem at all.

If you have a good idea or insight, please post it in the Kaggle discussions. It energizes the whole community, and it can even count toward medals for you (more on those below).

On the other hand:

- **Sharing on X (formerly Twitter), blogs, or other places outside Kaggle is not desirable.** Those aren't places where all participants can see things fairly, so they don't count as the "share with everyone" the rules envision. Keep useful information on the Kaggle discussions, not on external platforms.

## 2. No multiple accounts

**One person, one account—this is the fundamental rule.**

Using a sub-account (a second/alt account) to:

- manipulate your ranking,
- rack up extra submissions, or
- unfairly chase medals or prizes

is entirely prohibited. If discovered, it can lead to account suspension or disqualification. This isn't a temporary penalty—it's a serious one that can affect your entire account for a long time. Don't do it, not even "just a little."

---

## 3. About prizes and medals — the difference between the two tracks

PokeCa ABC has two tracks: the **Simulation Track** and the **Strategy Track**. **What you can earn differs by track**, so it's good to be clear about what you're aiming for.

| Track | Medals | Prize money |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Simulation Track | Yes | No |
| Strategy Track | No | Yes |

- **Medals in the Simulation Track** accumulate to raise your overall Kaggle ranking and can earn you titles (such as Expert / Master). They're a reward that stays with you as a Kaggler. There is no prize money.
- The **Strategy Track** doesn't come with medals, but it awards prize money.

Roughly speaking: "If you want to grow your ranking and titles" → Simulation Track; "If you want to go for prize money" → Strategy Track (and of course you're welcome to take on both). Note that receiving prize money may come with conditions such as identity verification, so please check the official rules page for details.
