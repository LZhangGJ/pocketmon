I went through the **5,333 most recent games** (2026-07-06 daily top episode dataset) and, for each game, pulled out which side held the first/second choice, what they chose, and who won. Sharing the numbers.

### 1. What the choosing side picks

The player who holds the choice picks **first** by a wide margin:

| Choice | Games | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Take first | 4,879 | 91.5% |
| Take second | 454 | 8.5% |

### 2. Win rate by turn order

And that preference looks correct — first wins more often:

| Turn order | Wins | Win rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| First | 2,939 | 55.2% |
| Second | 2,382 | 44.8% |

Going first is worth roughly **+10 points**.

### 3. Can you actually get the order you want?

The choice belongs to the coin-toss winner. Assuming a fair 50/50 toss and that the opponent behaves like the population above (takes first ~91.5% of the time), the probability of actually **ending up** with the order you want is:

| Order you want | Probability you get it |
|---|---:|
| Second | ~96% |
| First | ~54% |

- **Want second → you almost always get it.** If you win the toss you just pick it; and if you lose the toss, the opponent takes first (91.5%), which leaves you going second anyway.
- **Want first → basically luck.** If you lose the toss, the opponent takes first and you get pushed to second.

### 4. Discussion — does this matter competitively?

This creates an asymmetry worth thinking about:

- The **advantageous** order (first) is the one you **can't reliably secure** — it comes down to the coin toss (~54%).
- The **disadvantageous** order (second) is the one you **can almost always secure** (~96%), precisely because everyone fights over first.

Two implications:

1. **You can't engineer your way into the first-turn advantage.** No matter how much you value first, ~46% of the time you lose the toss and get put on second. A deck that "only works going first" is exposed to that variance every game.
2. **"Second" is the controllable state.** If a deck/strategy could actually fight well on the second turn, you could steer into your intended position ~96% of the time, converting coin-toss variance into consistency. In the current data second is clearly a disadvantage, so this is still a *hypothesis*, not a solution — **but second is one of the few positions you can reliably aim for**, so a build that's strong going second might be worth investing in.

*Note: win rates are pooled across all archetypes and agents.*

I'd be curious whether others see the same split, and whether anyone has tried a build designed around going second.
