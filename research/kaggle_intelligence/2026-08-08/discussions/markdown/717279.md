#### Message 3486769 — unknown

Posted: 2026-07-03T00:32:14.427Z

Found the issue. I had been assumed steps[i][player]['action'] is the response to that same step's steps[i][player]['observation']. That assumption turned out to be wrong more often than expected: for minCount==maxCount==1 decisions specifically, I measured ~20% of same-step (observation, action) pairs where the recorded action's index literally falls outside that step's own select.option range (or the count doesn't satisfy minCount/maxCount) — meaning the action cannot be a valid response to that step's select, full stop.

The fix: instead of trusting the same-step action, search forward from step i for the next step where that player's status == "ACTIVE" and action is non-empty, and treat that as the real response to step i's observation. (Steps 0/1 are the exception — the initial deck submission at step 1 is genuinely same-step-paired.)

I validated this by extracting the submitted tarball and replaying real recorded obs_dicts from 22 scored episodes (1,277 decision points, spanning several different opponent archetypes) through main.agent(). With naive same-step pairing there was a 35% mismatch rate. With the corrected forward-search pairing: 1,277/1,277 (100%) match, no exceptions, across every archetype.

So I don't believe there is  engine or build staleness — it was a bug in how I paired observations and actions when parsing the replay JSON.
