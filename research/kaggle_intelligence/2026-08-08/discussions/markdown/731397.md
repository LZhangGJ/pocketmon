This may introduce a cold-start problem, though: when a pretrained checkpoint uses a new deck to play against the same checkpoint, the results have extremely high variance. Fine-tuning for every new deck is also extremely computationally expensive and time-consuming, making it difficult to reliably evaluate a deck’s true strength.

you should think of it like this:  

1) let the placement of cards in active,bench, etc ... JUST BEFORE ATTACK be called FORMATION. It includes item, energy attachment, stadium  ... anything that can affect the damage you give to the opponent  

2) for a FORMATION, score it for damage, HP,  wish card gained by opp, no of cards used ...  
compute the time you need to build it (that is setup cost). e.g. if you have many cards, you may need many turns    

3) then you have a list of FORMATION and their cost and score   

4) you can always ask chatgpt for a stronger or customised FORMATION. Or copy from LB, etc ...

deck is to get good formation (strong, fast to build, resistance to random draw). some deck can change formation based on opponent's formation

then try to train your model to make the most powerful formation in the shortest time and verify it works by playing it with an opponent or self-play

Typically, the search space may be smaller than you think
