## Message 3507246 — unknown

Posted: 2026-08-01T17:05:08.687Z

I would imagine that the entries from the James Cox & Henry Chao duo (currently top 10) are rules-based agents.
They are both accomplished players in real-life play and I don't know that either of them have any history of RL or even ML. At a glance it looks like [their list is a tool box](https://x.com/ptcgjamesc/status/2072321980024357031/) - 'big basics' for attackers, but with retreat lock & wall options. I think it would be very feasible to write a strong rules-based agent for this strategy using GenAI, especially for a top-player.

In my opinion, rules-based agents can only outperform competent RL if they adopt an unconventional/underrepresented strategy. I think given the sheer volume of games available to train on now, that for conventional strategies e.g. Alakazam an RL model would have better handling of the various edge cases that can arise in the game than an evaluation rule codebase.

I don't know if the models people are training are as flexible as being able to play any given list of 60 cards at this stage, but that capability along with anticipating the underrepresented deck lists seems to me like it would be enough to crush rules-based agents across the board.

I am rooting for the Cox & Chao duo, but I think that they risk losing their edge as their agent volunteers increasingly more logs about how it plays into the public dataset. I wouldn't be surprised if their final submission is entirely different to their current one. In general I think any rules-based agents that end up performing well in the final standings will appear out of nowhere very close to the submission deadline.
