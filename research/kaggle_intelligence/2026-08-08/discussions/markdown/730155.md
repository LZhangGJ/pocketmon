> Hi everyone,
>
> I am trying to understand how strong agents handle information order within a
> turn. A common choice is whether to use a deterministic search action first
> or draw first.
>
> Searching first can thin the deck and avoid drawing a card that could have
> been searched, but drawing first can reveal what is missing and preserve the
> search action for a better target. The correct order also seems to depend on
> the current hand, known deck contents, once-per-turn actions, and whether the
> deck is search-heavy or draw-heavy.
>
> If you are comfortable sharing broad design choices:
>
> 1. Do you encode this as hand-written ordering rules, learn it from full-turn
>    sequences, or compare short action sequences with search/rollouts?
> 2. For behavior cloning, do you train each decision independently, or preserve
>    the complete order of actions within the turn?
> 3. How do you treat two action orders that reach a similar board but expose
>    different information along the way?
> 4. Have you found search-heavy decks easier or harder for learned policies
>    than draw-heavy decks?
