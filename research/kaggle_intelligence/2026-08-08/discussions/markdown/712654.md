Hi everyone,

I published a notebook that introduces card2vec — an AutoEncoder-based approach
to represent each Pokémon TCG card as a 64-dimensional dense vector.

🔗 https://www.kaggle.com/code/niiino/card2vec-learning-dense-card-embeddings

The motivation is that integer card IDs have two drawbacks:
- No generalization to unseen cards
- No semantic structure (similar cards aren't close in representation space)

card2vec encodes card attributes (HP, type, energy cost, effect keywords, etc.)
into a 148-dim feature vector, then compresses it to 64 dims via AutoEncoder.

⏱️ Runs in under 1 minute on CPU — feel free to try it!

---

I'd love to discuss a few open questions I have:

**1. Is dense card representation actually useful?**
Does replacing integer IDs with attribute-based embeddings meaningfully help
the agent? I've seen a slight improvement in my early experiments, but haven't
done enough validation to draw conclusions yet.

**2. Context-awareness**
word2vec derives meaning from context (surrounding words). card2vec only encodes
static attributes — it has no notion of how a card interacts with others in a
deck or game situation. I think this is a significant limitation, but I'm not
sure how to address it. Has anyone thought about this?

I'm fairly new to Kaggle and not highly confident in my ML knowledge,
so any feedback is very welcome!
