Ultra Ball and Boss's Orders are strong cards, but they're difficult to use.

In real Pokémon TCG play, almost 100% of decks run Boss's Orders. However, the judgment of when and how to use it is never really put into words. The easy case is obvious — when you can take your last Prize cards and close out the game (lethal). But there are many other uses:

Stalling for time — dragging up a Pokémon with a high retreat cost and low damage output to buy turns.
Removing a threat pre-emptively — knocking out a dangerous Pokémon while it's still in its pre-evolution stage.
Targeting an energized Pokémon — taking out a Pokémon that already has energy attached.
and so on — there are all sorts of ways to use it.

The same goes for Ultra Ball. It's a powerful card that can search for any Pokémon, but it costs two cards, and what to discard has to be decided flexibly depending on the board state and other factors. Just putting it in the deck doesn't make it work.
(An Alakazam deck that doesn't need Ultra Ball has it easy — lucky them.)

I think beginners would find this much easier to get into if there were reference implementations. Does anyone have a recommended approach?

English
Short version: for a search-based agent you usually don't "teach the triggers" at all  you let the search evaluate the card as one branch and price it by outcome. The cases you listed (lethal / stalling / removing a pre-evolution / hitting an energized target) are things a good evaluation discovers, not rules you write. Hard-coding them tends to fight the search and create brittle edge cases.

Where hand-crafting actually pays off is the opposite end pruning the action space, not encoding triggers. Boss's Orders (up to ~5 gust targets × a follow-up) and Ultra Ball (every 2-card discard combination) blow up the branching factor, so:

Ultra Ball discard: don't expand every C(n,2) combo. Score hand cards with a cheap "keep-value" heuristic (dead/redundant first: a 2nd Ultra Ball, cards you can't cast this turn, surplus copies) and only open the top 1–2 discard candidates as branches. Keep it a soft prior the search can override, not a hard rule.
Boss target: enumerate the gust as a real action and prune targets with a prior (a benched threat you can KO / retreat cost depending on whether you want tempo) but let whether to gust be decided by comparing rollout values.
The real lever is the value/rollout signal. "Kill the pre-evo now" pays off 2–3 turns later; if your leaf evaluation only counts Prizes, that payoff is invisible and you'll be tempted to hard-code it back in. Fix the evaluation instead.
On reference implementations: I don't think a clean "Boss's Orders module" exists in strong engines, because it's emergent, not a module. The pattern to copy is generic determinized-MCTS / ISMCTS: card = action, a policy prior for move ordering, a value net or rollout for evaluation. For the discard specifically, borrow the "card-utility as tiebreaker only" idea from MTG/Hearthstone bots.

Two lessons that cost me real debugging time, if you do hand-code any card-specific pruning:

Validate against the engine's real action structure, not what you assume it looks like. A single wrong assumption about the option format can make your rule silently do nothing.
Instrument it so you can tell "never fires" from "fires wrong." A green unit test won't catch an inert rule — only logging on real games will.
日本語
結論から言うと、探索ベースのエージェントなら「発動条件を教える」必要は基本的にありません。カードを1つの分岐として探索に評価させ、結果で価値づけすればいい。挙げられた使い方（リーサル／時間稼ぎ／進化前の処理／エネ付きを狙う）は、良い評価関数が自然に発見するもので、個別に書くルールではありません。ハードコードするとむしろ探索と喧嘩して、脆いエッジケースを生みます。

手作業が本当に効くのは逆側 — 発動条件ではなく行動空間の枝刈りです。ボス（最大5体ほどのgust対象×その後の攻撃）とハイパーボール（2枚トラッシュの全組み合わせ）は分岐を爆発させるので：

ハイパーボールのトラッシュ： C(n,2) を全部展開しない。手札を安い「キープ価値」ヒューリスティックで採点し（不要札から：2枚目のハイパーボール、そのターン使えない札、余剰コピー）、上位1〜2候補だけを分岐として開く。探索が上書きできるソフトな事前分布にとどめ、ハードルールにしない。
ボスの対象： gust を実アクションとして列挙し、対象を事前分布で枝刈り（倒せるベンチの脅威／テンポ次第で逃げエネの重さ）。ただしgust するか否かはロールアウト値の比較で決めさせる。
本当のカギは価値／ロールアウトの信号。 「進化前を今倒す」は2〜3ターン後に効いてくる。葉の評価がサイドしか見ていないとその見返りは不可視で、結局ハードコードに戻したくなる。評価関数のほうを直すべき。
参考実装について：強いエンジンに綺麗な「ボスの指令モジュール」は無いと思います。モジュールではなく創発だからです。真似すべきは一般的な determinized-MCTS / ISMCTS：カード＝アクション、着手順のための policy prior、評価は value 網かロールアウト。トラッシュ選択に限れば、MTG／ハースストーンのボットの「カード有用度はタイブレークにのみ使う」考え方が流用できます。

カード固有の枝刈りをハードコードするなら、実際にデバッグで痛い目を見た教訓を2つ：

想定ではなくエンジンの実際のアクション構造に対して検証する。 オプション形式の思い込みが1つあるだけで、ルールが何もせず黙って空振りすることがある。
計測を仕込む — 「一度も発動しない」と「誤発動」を区別できるように。緑のユニットテストは不活性なルールを捕まえられない。実戦のログだけが捕まえる。

I have also struggled with this, especially ultra ball. There's often situations where you shouldn't play it since the best card you can find isn't worth the worst two cards in your hand. Determining that with search is hard (since the issues usually show themselves over a multi-turn horizon) and it's hard to simply define the reasoning as to why an ultra ball usage is "bad". 

Boss's orders in my experience is a lot easier to write rules for. Can we get a KO on a benched but not the active? Possibly worth using it. Are we going to get KO'd next turn? Possibly worth using it defensively. In my experience you can get boss's orders usage / targeting "good enough" without search, although even very basic search can help a lot.
