While working on my battle agent, I began wondering what we actually mean by “Pokémon TCG intelligence.”

A strong agent and an intelligent agent may not necessarily be the same thing.

For example, an agent might achieve good results by using:

・highly detailed rules written for one specific deck,
・matchup-specific scripts,
・strong action priorities,
・large numbers of hard-coded exceptions.

Such an agent may be very effective. But does it understand Pokémon TCG, or has it simply learned a sufficiently complete procedure for a narrow environment?

I started thinking about this after observing several kinds of mistakes in my own agent.

For example:

・It has a card that can reliably search for a needed Basic Pokémon, but uses a draw Supporter first.
・Its Active Pokémon can attack this turn, but it does not prepare another attacker for the following turn.
・It sees the opponent’s board, but does not connect that information to threat recognition or defensive action.
・It evaluates each legal action individually, but does not form a plan for the entire turn.

In these cases, the agent knows the card effects and can perform legal actions. The missing ability seems to exist somewhere between card knowledge and action selection.

Perhaps Pokémon TCG intelligence includes abilities such as:

1. State interpretation
Understanding not only which cards are in play, but what roles they currently have: attacker, evolution target, draw engine, future resource, threat, and so on.

2.Requirement generation
Inferring what conditions must be established now in order to attack, survive, or continue the game on later turns.

3.Temporal planning
Evaluating not only the current action, but the sequence of actions across the turn and across future turns.

4.Threat reasoning
Predicting what the opponent may do next and identifying which parts of our future plan they can disrupt.

5.Planning under uncertainty
Maintaining several possible lines while accounting for hidden cards, prizes, draws, and incomplete knowledge of the opponent’s deck.

6.Replanning
Updating the plan whenever new cards are drawn or new information becomes available.

7.Generalization
Applying these abilities to new cards, decks, or situations without requiring a completely new handwritten script.

A provisional definition might be:

Pokémon TCG intelligence is the ability to interpret cards, board states, opponents, and uncertainty; identify the conditions required for victory; and generate, execute, and revise plans that preserve those conditions over time.

This also raises a broader question about the competition.

What should count as a Pokémon TCG AI?

・An agent that plays one deck extremely well?
・An agent that can operate many decks?
・An agent that can adapt to previously unseen cards?
・A system that also performs deck building and metagame analysis?
・Or simply any artificial agent capable of playing legal games?

I do not think there is a single correct answer. A specialized rule-based agent may be both technically impressive and competitively successful.

Still, separating strength, knowledge, planning, and generalization may help us better understand what kinds of systems we are building.

I would be interested to hear how other participants think about this:

What abilities would an agent need before you would say that it “understands” Pokémon TCG?

(This is not intended to propose a single definition or evaluation criterion. I thought it might be useful to have one open thread where participants can share what they are trying to build, and what they personally mean by “understanding” Pokémon TCG.)
