## Pokemon Moves

While looking at the move combinations in Pokémon cards, a thought suddenly occurred to me:

“Isn’t this structure somewhat similar to a **mathematical theory**?”

If so, perhaps playing cards is not simply about using moves.   
It could also be seen as the process of developing and unfolding a mathematical theory.

## Representing Moves as State Transitions

For example, **Scratch** increases the damage on the opponent's Active Pokémon by 20.

$$ d'_s(p) = d_s(p) + 20 $$

**Trade** is a composition of two operations: discarding one card and then drawing two cards.

$$ \mathrm{Trade} = \mathrm{Draw}_2 \circ \mathrm{Discard}_1 $$

The composition operator `∘` indicates that the operation on the right is executed first. Therefore, one card is discarded first, and then two cards are drawn.

A combo consists of executing a sequence of such state transitions.

$$ s_0 \xrightarrow{a_1} s_1 \xrightarrow{a_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{a_n} s_n $$  



Then, what does it really mean for two players to compete against each other in a Pokémon battle?

Could general mathematical theories be represented through competitive card-game battles?

It would be fascinating if reinforcement learning and MCTS could also be used to develop mathematical theory.
