# Tracking 3,057 teams through 6 weeks of meta: who switched decks, when — and why it was always too late

We've seen a one-day meta snapshot (Pilkwang Kim's Jul 18 notebook) and a great analysis of *how* top agents are implemented from decision timing ("Top players' methods, revealed by 30,000 games"). What I haven't seen is the meta treated as a **dynamical system**: tracking individual teams over time to see who switches decks, to what, and how fast the field punishes them for it.

So I downloaded 14 of the official [Daily Top Episodes datasets](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/kaggle/pokemon-tcg-ai-battle-episodes-index) (every 3rd day, Jun 17 → Jul 26), classified **every episode** in them (74,634 games, no sampling) into deck archetypes by signature cards, and traced **3,057 unique teams** across the snapshots. In the Sankey below, node height = archetype share of games that day; ribbons = **actual deck switches** (a team appearing in two consecutive snapshots with a different modal archetype).

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-forum-message-attachments/o/inbox%2F1601218%2Facd68d9d687deead420ea461156f092f%2Fmeta_sankey.png?generation=1785142266784452&alt=media)

## 1. Four eras in six weeks

- **Crustle/Lucario era** (mid-June): Crustle 37%, Lucario 28% on Jun 17.
- **The Archaludon bubble** (Jun 26 – Jul 2): see below.
- **Alakazam plateau** (early–mid July): peaks at 46% on Jul 14, drifting ~50% winrate the whole time.
- **Grimmsnarl takeover** (Jul 17 →): 17% → 31% → 39% → **51.3% of all top-band games on Jul 26**. The #1 team and most of the current top-25 play it.

Meanwhile Mega Lucario ex — 28% of the top band on Jun 17 — is **extinct**: exactly 1 team still played it on Jul 26 (0.03% of games). Its field winrate had been sliding 47% → 35% before everyone abandoned it.

## 2. The Archaludon bubble is a cautionary tale about copying the leaderboard

Archaludon's timeline, share of games / winrate vs field (mirrors excluded):

| | 6/23 | 6/26 | 6/29 | 7/02 | 7/05 | 7/08 | 7/17 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| share | 0% | 9% | **41%** | 13% | 3% | 3% | ~0% |
| WR vs field | 45% | **65%** | 55% | 48% | 31% | 48% | 26% |

It won 65% while nobody played it, tripled its share in three days (the Sankey shows the inflow coming mostly from Lucario and Crustle players), and the moment it became the field, its edge evaporated — then it crashed to near-zero share within a week. Adoption lags performance by roughly one dataset interval (~3 days), and by the time a deck is *visibly* dominant on the leaderboard, its expected value is already regressing.

## 3. Grimmsnarl has now reached the same saturation point

Grimmsnarl won **61–67%** vs the field in late June at 2–29% share. As its share climbed to 51%, its non-mirror winrate converged to **50.3%** — it is now at equilibrium with the field it created. The pooled matchup matrix for Jul 17–26 (row beats column, N in parens) shows why it still holds:

| row ¥ col | grimmsnarl | alakazam | tarountula | garchomp | crustle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **grimmsnarl** | – | **57%** (3,703) | 57% (1,237) | *41%* (993) | 50% (830) |
| **alakazam** | 43% | – | *28%* (1,038) | **64%** (916) | 60% (978) |
| **tarountula** | 43% | **72%** | – | 44% (316) | 57% (237) |
| **garchomp** | **59%** | 36% | 56% | – | 57% (233) |

That's a clean cycle: **Grimmsnarl > Alakazam > Garchomp > Grimmsnarl**, with Tarountula as a dedicated Alakazam-hunter (72%!) that loses to the other two. Grimmsnarl dominates because it beats the *most popular* deck (Alakazam) while its own counter (Garchomp, 59% against it) is only at 7% share. If history repeats, Garchomp — currently the highest-EV deck against the actual field mix — is the next bubble candidate.

## 4. The top band itself is a revolving door

Across the 14 snapshots I tracked 3,057 unique teams. **Not a single team appears in all 14.** Only 1,492 appear in ≥2 snapshots at all. Between adjacent snapshots, 15–92% of teams carry over (56–71% in the stabler late-July period). Sustained residency at the top appears to be much harder than reaching it once — consistent with the rating-variance discussions elsewhere on this forum.

## Method notes

- Dataset inclusion is Kaggle's own criterion (episodes ranked by average participant rating, ~4.5–7.8k games/day), so "top band" = roughly the top few dozen teams plus whoever they queued into; ratings themselves are not in the replay JSONs.
- Archetypes are classified by signature card IDs (e.g. Marnie's Grimmsnarl ex = 648); a team's archetype for a day is its modal archetype; winner read from final rewards.

## Reproduce it yourself

Attach one or more daily datasets from the [episodes index](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/kaggle/pokemon-tcg-ai-battle-episodes-index) to a notebook and run this (each attached day adds a column to the Sankey):

```python
import json, glob
from collections import Counter, defaultdict
import plotly.graph_objects as go

SIGS = {"alakazam": {743, 245}, "archaludon": {190, 170, 840}, "grimmsnarl": {648},
        "starmie": {1031, 361}, "trevenant": {879}, "dragapult": {121},
        "crustle": {345, 533}, "bellibolt": {269}, "abomasnow": {723, 419},
        "lucario": {678}, "dudunsparce": {306, 997, 66},
        "garchomp": {380, 381}, "gardevoir": {747}, "tarountula": {400, 401}}

def archetype(deck):
    s = set(deck)
    return next((a for a, sig in SIGS.items() if sig & s), "other")

def decks(d):  # both 60-card lists live in step0 visualize[0].action
    for p in d["steps"][0]:
        for viz in p.get("visualize") or []:
            found = [x for x in (viz.get("action") or []) if isinstance(x, list) and len(x) == 60]
            if len(found) == 2:
                return found
    return None

games = []  # (date, team0, arch0, team1, arch1)
for ddir in sorted(glob.glob("/kaggle/input/pokemon-tcg-ai-battle-episodes-*")):
    date = ddir[-10:]
    for fp in glob.glob(f"{ddir}/*.json"):
        try:
            d = json.load(open(fp))
            dk = decks(d)
            if not dk:
                continue
            t = (d.get("info") or {}).get("TeamNames") or ["?", "?"]
            games.append((date, t[0], archetype(dk[0]), t[1], archetype(dk[1])))
        except Exception:
            pass
print(len(games), "games parsed")

dates = sorted({g[0] for g in games})
share, modal = {}, {}
for dt in dates:
    day = [g for g in games if g[0] == dt]
    ctr = Counter(a for g in day for a in (g[2], g[4]))
    tot = sum(ctr.values())
    share[dt] = {a: 100 * c / tot for a, c in ctr.items()}
    td = defaultdict(Counter)
    for g in day:
        td[g[1]][g[2]] += 1
        td[g[3]][g[4]] += 1
    modal[dt] = {t: c.most_common(1)[0][0] for t, c in td.items()}

flows = Counter()  # ribbons = teams switching archetypes between snapshots
for d1, d2 in zip(dates, dates[1:]):
    for t in modal[d1].keys() & modal[d2].keys():
        flows[(d1, modal[d1][t], modal[d2][t])] += 1

nid, lab, xs = {}, [], []
for i, dt in enumerate(dates):
    for a, s in sorted(share[dt].items(), key=lambda x: -x[1]):
        if s >= 2:
            nid[(dt, a)] = len(lab)
            lab.append(f"{a} {s:.0f}%")
            xs.append(0.02 + 0.96 * i / max(1, len(dates) - 1))
src, dst, val = [], [], []
for (d1, a, b), n in flows.items():
    d2 = dates[dates.index(d1) + 1]
    if (d1, a) in nid and (d2, b) in nid:
        src.append(nid[(d1, a)]); dst.append(nid[(d2, b)]); val.append(n)
go.Figure(go.Sankey(arrangement="snap",
    node=dict(label=lab, x=xs, pad=6, thickness=12),
    link=dict(source=src, target=dst, value=val))).update_layout(
    height=650, title="Top-band archetype evolution (ribbons = team deck switches)").show()
```
