There are three threads open right now about leaderboard variance, so here is the arithmetic, and then a way to get the games without spending submissions.

A win rate measured over n games has a standard error of sqrt(p(1-p)/n). Near an even matchup that is 0.5/sqrt(n):

| games | one standard error | rough 95 percent band |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | 9.1 points | plus or minus 18 |
| 100 | 5.0 points | plus or minus 10 |
| 400 | 2.5 points | plus or minus 5 |
| 2500 | 1.0 point | plus or minus 2 |

So a 30 game test cannot resolve anything smaller than about an 18 point difference, and a 100 game test cannot resolve anything smaller than 10. If you change one heuristic and your win rate goes from 0.52 to 0.57 over 100 games, you have not measured an improvement. You have measured noise that happened to point the right way.

The ladder is worse than this table, not better. Your opponents change while you are being measured, the pool is not uniform, and your rating is a moving average over a moving population. That is enough to explain most of what the variance threads are describing without anything being broken.

The useful part is that you do not need the ladder to play games. The engine ships with the competition and runs full games in a plain CPU notebook, so you can run the hundreds of games the table asks for against any opponent you choose. Two numbers from a run I did that way, both from a single CPU session: a simple rule based agent against the official random sample agent was 0.917 over 60 games with an average of 6.5 turns, and the same agent against a copy of itself was 0.500 over 30 games. The mirror sitting on 0.500 is the sanity check that the harness itself is not biased, and it is worth running for your own setup before you trust anything else it tells you.

One more thing that shows up immediately once you run games locally. If your agent ever returns an option index the engine did not offer, the call raises, and that game is gone. Count those separately from losses, because a rate of a few percent is invisible on the ladder and will quietly cap your rating. The same goes for deck rejection: `battle_start` hands back an error object instead of an observation, and reading its `errorType` tells you which rule you broke rather than leaving you to guess.

The harness I used, with the seat alternation and the error counting already in it, is here if it saves you the setup: https://www.kaggle.com/code/busyaprime/test-your-agent-a-local-matchup-harness

Another thing that I've found super useful has been running a bunch of local matches with seeded RNG (running with modified engine source). It makes it much easier to compare between runs since a change that shouldn't affect a particular matchup has literally the same result as opposed to approximately same result within noise.
