Strong +1 to this thread. A concrete suggestion:

The core issue with per-match rating updates (Elo/TrueSkill-style) is that the result is path-dependent — the final rating depends on match order and is easily swayed by a few recent games, which matters a lot in a game as high-variance as PTCG.

Instead, I'd suggest decoupling matchmaking from rating estimation:

 - Rating: fit a Bradley–Terry model to the entire match history at once. This is order-independent, uses every game equally, and is cheap to recompute (seconds even on a huge number of games). Publishing uncertainty (posterior SD / CI) alongside would be a nice bonus.
 - Matchmaking: pick the pairs whose outcomes are most informative for the estimation — in practice, pairs with predicted win probability near 50%, prioritizing agents with few games / high uncertainty.

This doesn't require more games or compute — only the scheduling policy and the aggregation change.

On the "too disruptive to existing expectations" concern raised in the other post — I'd gently push back on applying it here. What participants are actually expecting is that the final standings reflect agent strength; the current rating noise is what's disrupting that expectation. A batch BT fit doesn't change the game, the rules, or anyone's strategy — it just measures the same games better. My guess is that most participants would welcome it, and I suspect it's the outcome the hosts want too: a leaderboard everyone can trust. And it's encouraging that Bradley–Terry is already planned for Kaggriculture — this competition seems like a natural place to apply the same idea, at minimum as a recomputation of the final standings over the full match history.

Thanks for considering, and thanks for running the competition!
