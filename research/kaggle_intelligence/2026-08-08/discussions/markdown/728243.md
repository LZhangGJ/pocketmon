After reading through several posts, the discussion suggests that leaderboard performance may be influenced by several interacting factors:

* Too few games after the rating begins to stabilize
* High volatility during a submission’s early matches
* Large variance across decks and matchups
* Rating-based matchmaking that may produce different opponent paths

Some competitors reported that nearly identical agents reached both **1100+** and the **800s**. A 20,000-match simulation also suggested that best-of-three evaluation was substantially more stable than best-of-one, while the number of episodes received by different submissions reportedly varied by **6–10×**.

Rather than treating this only as a rating-system issue, the practical question is how competitors should adapt their evaluation and submission strategy.

**Possible approaches:**

1. Protect a proven submission instead of replacing it too quickly.
2. Require hundreds of fresh, seat-balanced local games against the current best agent, and confirm improvements with a second independent run.
3. Test against a broad panel of elite decks, counter-decks, public agents, and lower-rated agents. Require a meaningful improvement with a positive confidence lower bound, rather than relying on raw win rate.
4. Use duplicate identical submissions only as a controlled variance experiment, analyzing their pooled games rather than selecting the luckier score.
5. Avoid repeatedly restarting strong agents unless the expected improvement is large enough to justify the uncertainty.

This may mean that leaderboard success depends on both building a stronger agent and designing a disciplined process for validation, submission timing, and risk management.

**How are you adapting to this variance?**

* What local promotion gate do you use?
* How many games are enough before submitting?
* Do you protect a strong existing submission?
* Have you tested identical submissions to estimate variance?
* Do you evaluate against low-, mid-, and high-rating opponent pools separately?
